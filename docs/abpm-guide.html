For process owners

What changes when part of your process is done by an AI agent, what you can still fix, and what you can see.

Fix the outcome, free the strategy

A person follows a procedure. A program follows it exactly. An AI agent reaches the result its own way, and not the same way twice. Writing a longer procedure does not change that.

So AWA asks you for something else: say what must be true of the result. The estimate is a number. It is within the adjuster's authority. Every damaged part is priced. The agent is then free in how it gets there, and a result that does not meet what you said is refused, retried, or handed to a person. You decide which.

Decisions you can read

Routine cases are decided by a table you can read and sign off: rows of conditions, one outcome each, and a declared policy for what happens when two rows match. The cases no row covers can go to a model, which may only choose among options you listed. If its answer is not one of them, or it is not confident enough, the case takes the default path. The engine never invents a route.

The customer's journey, not just your process

A customer does not experience a process. They experience a year: they buy, they claim, they renew. A journey in AWA is that year, made of stages with a time allowed for each, goals, and the ways a customer can leave.

It records where the customer and your organisation meet, over which channel and system, and what changed hands: the contract, the invoice, the receipt, the certificate. It also carries an estimate of how the customer feels. That estimate is the one thing here that could mislead, so every point on it says whether the customer told you (a survey answer) or whether a rule of yours assumed it (a long wait is felt). A view of a journey has to show the difference.

Why look at several journeys at once: in the example, a claim is paid in full and its journey completes. The three-day wait in the middle shows up five months later, in another journey, as a policy that is not renewed.

Open the journey view.

Knowing who is stuck

A stage that lasts longer than you allowed is marked stalled. So is a stage whose workflow failed. A stalled journey is not a failed one: it is a customer nobody is working for, and a reason for a person to look. awa journey list --stalled lists them.

Changing a model or a rule

Before you change a model, a prompt, a contract or a decision rule, replay your recorded runs under the change. Everything else is held as it was recorded. You get, per run and per step: the same, changed, improved, regressed. A run that used to complete and no longer does is a regression, and the command says so with its exit status.

Where the time goes

The engine measures process time, waiting time and lead time per step, and classifies waste by the DOWNTIME categories. In a journey most of the time is waiting, and most of that is waiting for the customer or for a person: the view states those gaps in words rather than drawing them to scale.
