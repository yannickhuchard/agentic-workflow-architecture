Three findings

  1. 1

    Switch is a long silence

    All 7 households who reached Switch heard nothing for a week or more, a median of 14 days, the longest 21.

  2. 2

    People step in six times

    Four people decided at six steps, after a median wait of 3 hours. Three of those tasks first went to someone the policy then refused.

  3. 3

    The safeguards held

    The agents met 33 of 37 outcome contracts; the 4 misses went back to the agent or to a person. Guardrails acted 3 times: 1 redaction and 2 blocks.

06 Recommendations

What to change, in order

8 recommendations, computed from the record by twelve rules and the journey’s options; each with its evidence, its owner and how its effect is known.

Mark size: customers concerned. Shape: the kind. Impact = customers concerned ÷ 8 × severity; effort 1 to 3.

  1. 1

    1. Tell customers where their switch stands

    Every customer who reached Switch heard nothing for 7 days or more, a median of 14 days; Sofia waited longest, 21 days. Mood fell in Switch 3 times for Rowan and Sofia.

    Action. Add a touchpoint “Switch update”: a short message on day 7 of the switch, and at once when the registry is late. The letters agent writes it under the existing guardrail.

    experience · Switch · Switching team · impact 1.75, effort 2 · Estimate: No customer would go 7 days without a word in Switch (7 of 7 now). The change adds a touchpoint the recorded runs never had, so a replay cannot show it (REPLAY_UNRECORDED). Measured after the change by the longest silence and the mood at the end of Switch.

  2. 2

    2. Hand an unreadable document to a person at the first failure

    At Verify documents the outcome contract handed one run to a person (Joaquim); At Verify installation documents the outcome contract handed one run to a person (Tomás), each after a second attempt that failed as the first had. A service level of the step was breached while it waited.

    Action. Set the outcome contract budget of Verify documents and Verify installation documents to one attempt (budgets.max_attempts: 1) when a document cannot be read or a guardrail blocked it, so the person gets it at once.

    quality · Verification · Credit team and Grid connections team · impact 0.5, effort 1 · Replay: Counterfactual replay with ai: recorded on the 2 runs concerned: attempts at the step 4 → 2, 2 failed attempts fewer, about 7 minutes of agent time fewer (the recorded durations of the attempts not made; the replay supplies the step's recorded result, so these figures come from the recorded attempts). The same path and the same people deciding; no regression. Right first time is unchanged: an escalation still counts.

  3. 3

    3. Send each task to someone allowed to decide it

    Three tasks went first to a person the policy then refused: hana at Grid operator approval (separation of duties); gabriel at Grid operator approval, two engineers (four eyes); marco at Credit referral (no rule). Each refusal was right; the routing was not.

    Action. Offer a task only to the people the policy allows: credit referrals to credit officers, and grid approvals to an engineer who neither resolved the file nor approved it already.

    trust · Verification · Credit team and Grid connections team · impact 0.38, effort 1 · Estimate: 3 refusals fewer, the same people deciding. A replay holds people's work as recorded, so it cannot show the routing; the policy's own decisions in the record say who was allowed.

  4. 4

    4. Call before the plan offer lapses, on day 7

    Rowan Aldridge did not answer “plan.confirmed” in 14 days; the run took the timeout path to Confirm plan by phone. The stage Energy choices outlasted its time. Rowan's mood fell there.

    Action. Bring the phone call forward: wait 7 days for “plan.confirmed” before Confirm plan by phone, not 14.

    speed · Energy choices · Customer care · impact 0.25, effort 1 · Estimate: Rowan's Energy choices about 7 days shorter (14.2 → about 7.2 days), the journey about 23.6 days instead of 30.6. A replay holds timeouts as recorded, so this is an estimate.

  5. 5

    5. Remind on day 3, not day 7

    Joaquim needed a reminder after 7 days; Joaquim's Application took 9 days where the median is 5.1 hours. The reminder itself lowered the mood.

    Action. Send the reminder after 3 days, and let it say exactly which document is missing.

    experience · Application · Customer care · impact 0.25, effort 1 · Estimate: Up to 4 days shorter for a customer who answers the reminder as Joaquim did. Measured by the share of journeys reminded and the time in Application.

  6. 6

    6. Chase the registry before the switch times out

    “switch.completed” never came for Sofia Lind. After 21 days the wait ran out and the run failed; what it had done was undone. Nobody followed up in between.

    Action. Add a timer on the switch wait at day 14 that opens a task for the switching team to chase the registry, before the 21-day deadline.

    speed · Switch · Switching team · impact 0.38, effort 2 · Estimate: A chance to save the run that failed. A replay cannot show it: the new task has no recording (REPLAY_UNRECORDED). Measured by how often “switch.completed” times out.

  7. 7

    7. Offer a payment plan before declining

    Daniel Brooks: declined on an energy debt above the limit and told by post. The journey ends there, the only customer lost at Application.

    Action. Above the debt limit, offer a payment plan with a credit officer before the decline letter.

    experience · Application · Credit team · impact 0.38, effort 3 · Pilot: Not measurable by replay: the path is new. Run it as a pilot and measure the customers who leave at Application.

  8. 8

    8. Try a lighter model for clear document checks

    Verify documents uses 53.7% of all model tokens (49,070 of 91,459). The period cost EUR 0.46 in models and tools, EUR 0.057 per journey, below the materiality of EUR 1.00 per journey.

    Action. Replay the recorded runs with a lighter model tier for the clear cases, and keep the stronger tier when the agent's confidence is low.

    cost · Verification · Credit team · impact 0.25, effort 2 · Replay, not run: A replay with ai: live runs the agent again on the recorded runs and compares the contracts met. Not run yet.

No recommendation for Grid export, Contract, Active: every check passed.

Glossary

Metering point
The point where the grid measures the electricity a home takes and gives back; a supplier needs it to supply the home. https://github.com/yannickhuchard/agentic-workflow-architecture/blob/main/examples/electricity-onboarding/INTEGRATION.md#lookup_metering_point
EAN code
In many European markets, the 18-digit number that names a metering point; this example calls it metering_point_id, and a utility maps it to the number its market uses. Example
Prosumer
A customer who also produces electricity at home, for example with solar panels, and sends what they do not use to the grid. Example
Export tariff
The price per kWh the supplier pays a prosumer for the electricity the home sends to the grid. Example
Grid export registration
The child workflow that checks the installation's documents, gets the grid operator's approval and registers the export meter and tariff. Example
Supplier switch
The move of a home's supply from its current supplier to the new one, which the registry of metering points carries out and confirms. Example
Direct debit mandate
The customer's signed permission for the supplier to collect each bill from their bank account. Example
Commissioning report
The installer's report that a home installation, such as solar panels, was set up and tested, with its serial numbers and rated capacity. https://github.com/yannickhuchard/agentic-workflow-architecture/blob/main/examples/electricity-onboarding/INTEGRATION.md
Journey
The way one customer, order or other subject travels with the organisation, stage by stage, moved on by business events. §19
Stage
One part of a journey, with an owner and the time it is given. §19.2
Touchpoint
A moment where the customer and the organisation meet: a form, a call, a letter, a portal. §19.8
Mood
How the customer seems to feel on the journey's scale: stated by the customer, or inferred by a rule the journey declares. §19.9
Run
One execution of a workflow, recorded step by step in its journal. §5.1
Outcome contract
What must be true of a result, whatever route the agent took to it; a result that breaks it is tried again or refused. §11
Guardrail
A check on what goes into or comes out of an AI agent or a tool; when it finds something, it warns, redacts, blocks, asks for another attempt or hands the case to a person. §31
Separation of duties
A rule that stops the same principal from doing two steps that must have different hands, such as submitting a request and approving it. §30.3
Four eyes
A rule that a step needs the approval of two or more different people before the work goes on. §30.3
Service level
How long the work should take and the longest it may take; the run records a warning, then a breach. §23
Undo step
An activity that undoes the effect of an earlier one when the run fails, so that nothing is left half done. §13.6
Counterfactual replay
A recorded run run again with one thing changed, such as a contract or a rule, everything else held as recorded, and the two compared step by step. §20
Recommendation
A change to the journey or the work that a rule found in the record, shown with the numbers that fired it, the customers behind it, its owner and how its effect is known. View
Impact
How much a recommendation matters: the share of customers it concerns times how much it costs them, 3 for a journey lost, 2 for a stage or a promise outlasted or a fall of mood, 1 for friction. View
Effort
What a recommendation takes: 1 for a change of options, policy or a contract budget, 2 for a workflow change such as a timer or a touchpoint, 3 for new work for people. View
Right first time
The share of ended runs that completed with no failed step, no retry and no undo step. §21.4

Method

The measures come from awa-kpis.js, the same code as awa measures and the measures page; every figure carries its count.

The customers’ words are written from the record by the dossier’s narrator: each sentence cites the journal records it rests on, no sentence states a feeling the record does not state.

Recommendations come from rules R1 to R12 and the journey’s options; their effects are replayed where AWA can (specification section 20), estimated where it cannot, and marked.
